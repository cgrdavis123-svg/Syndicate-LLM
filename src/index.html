A language model, built from scratch, learning to write in your browser.

The same kind of network behind modern AI assistants, just tiny: 62,000 parameters instead of billions. Every layer and every derivative is written by hand in plain JavaScript, and it’s training right now on your CPU cores. It starts out knowing nothing. Watch the text.

      Backprop, by hand
      
      
      No autograd. The backward pass for layer norm, attention, GELU, the linear layers and the tied output head is derived on paper and written out. Every tensor’s gradient is checked against finite differences to better than one part in 100,000 before anything ships.
      
    
      
    
      
      
      Your cores as GPUs
      
      
      Training is synchronous data parallel, the way big models train across GPUs: each Web Worker gets the weights, samples its own batch, and sends back gradients. The page averages them, clips them, and takes an AdamW step with warm-up and cosine decay.
      
    
      
    
      
      
      Fast enough in plain JavaScript
      
      
      Weights are transposed so every matrix multiply is a dot product with its running sum in a register. GELU uses its one-exp sigmoid form. A separate worker writes the samples and draws what it’s looking at, so the page never stalls.
      
    
      
    
      
      
      Honest about size
      
      
      This is the architecture idea, not the scale. Real assistants have billions of parameters, read trillions of words and train for weeks on thousands of GPUs. This one reads a few hundred thousand characters for a few minutes. Shakespeare text is public domain; nothing you paste leaves the tab.
